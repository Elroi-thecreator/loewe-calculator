Understanding Loewe Additivity & Isobologram Analysis

A reference framework for pharmacodynamic synergy and combination therapy evaluation

The Mathematical Equation

The Loewe Additivity Model (introduced by Sigmund Loewe) assesses whether two agents act synergistically, additively, or antagonistically based on the sham combination principle. The Combination Index (CI) is computed as:

CI = (dA / DA) + (dB / DB)
  • dA, dB: Concentration of Drug A and Drug B in the combination.
  • DA, DB: Equipotent concentration of Drug A and Drug B alone producing the same biological effect (e.g., IC₅₀ or EC₅₀).

Loewe Additivity vs. Bliss Independence

While Bliss Independence assumes drugs act through completely independent biological mechanisms, Loewe Additivity is the gold standard when compounds act on the same pathway or shared biological targets.

An Isobologram provides a visual verification: the diagonal line connects standalone doses $(D_A, 0)$ and $(0, D_B)$. Synergy coordinates fall strictly below the line.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do I interpret my Combination Index (CI) score?

CI < 0.9 denotes synergism (the drugs enhance each other's potency), 0.9 ≤ CI ≤ 1.1 denotes simple Loewe dose addition, and CI > 1.1 indicates antagonism (the drugs interfere with one another).

Can this tool be used for cancer cell viability and antimicrobial checkerboard assays?

Yes. You can input IC₅₀, MIC (Minimum Inhibitory Concentration), or any predefined iso-effective concentration from in vitro or in vivo experiments.
